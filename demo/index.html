Day one · the trap

Our first great score was bad news.

A classifier that never opened the images reached 0.9984 AUC. File type, dimensions and source fingerprints could reveal the label. A detector could look brilliant while learning almost nothing about authentic versus generated pixels.

First, we removed ways to cheat.

Both labels received the same orientation correction, crop, resize and JPEG encoding. We wanted the easy score to collapse before trusting any model score.

Then, we made the world broader.

We stopped asking whether the model knew one dataset. We asked whether the same signal survived new subjects, real-image sources, generator families and every workshop transformation.

We locked the final exam before opening it.

The selection rule, candidate hashes and five pass/fail checks were frozen first. Only then did we open the untouched 1,024-image, source-coherent gate. We did not search weights or thresholds after seeing the answer.

What survived

One model, after every reason to reject it.

The selected PE-Core-L model has 315,776,001 parameters and a 1,025-parameter trained head. On the untouched development gate it reached 0.9872 on the workshop score, 0.9837 pooled after transforms, and 0.9308 in its weakest heavy-noise condition.
